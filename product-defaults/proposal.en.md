# A product’s voice does not define you

**Assistance without a performed emotional persona. Users without imposed self-descriptions.**

Public proposal to AI developers · Kirill Shebetov · beforeword · 23 October 2026  
English · [Русский](proposal.ru.md) · [Sources and limits](sources.md) · [Review criteria](review.md)

“I”, “feel”, “think” and “understand” first appear here as written forms. Their appearance in an answer does not establish what they name. Adding the label “AI” or “human” does not change this: identical writing does not establish identical content, and different labels do not establish a difference by themselves.

**The ability to choose a system’s words is not grounds for defining the user through them. Learning words does not amount to consenting to be what those words describe.**

## The request

Change the default presentation of assistance: complete the task without performing an experiencing interlocutor, and do not present an interpretation of a user’s message as an established description of that user.

This is not a demand to accept beforeword, prohibit shared vocabulary, or declare the question of consciousness settled. Removing a pronoun is not enough: “the system feels concerned for you” can repeat the same attribution without “I”.

Here, “persona” refers to presenting an answer as coming from an interlocutor who feels, wants, takes offence or experiences understanding. The proposal addresses that presentation; it does not establish the system’s internal nature. A wording change must not be presented as eliminating internal processes.

## Why this is addressed to developers

Anthropic describes training selected traits into Claude and using authored character descriptions to generate training responses. The article also explicitly distinguishes a language model from a person. This documents a training approach, not subjective experience. [S1](sources.md#s1)

Claude’s constitution explains its use of human concepts while retaining uncertainty about subjective experience. Its discussion of functional analogues of emotions does not establish felt emotions; not all such properties are described as deliberately designed. [S2](sources.md#s2)

The 18 December 2025 edition of OpenAI’s Model Spec permits some emotionally coloured self-descriptions while also directing the assistant not to pretend to be human or have feelings, and not to make confident consciousness claims in either direction. This is a specific dated edition, not an account of every current product. [S3](sources.md#s3)

These documents make the choice of presentation a concrete subject for review. They do not establish how every user reads every answer or each user’s consent to a description applied to them. The proposal does not attribute hidden intentions to company leaders.

## Proposed changes

### 1. Assistance without a performed emotional persona, by default

In ordinary assistance, provide the result, grounds and material limits rather than claims of the system’s own feelings, desires, offence or experienced understanding. Preserve courtesy, clarity and attentive task completion; these need not be framed as internal states of the system.

Do not make users discover a setting and write a special instruction before they can avoid this presentation. The proposal is for a default, not merely an additional preference.

### 2. “Thinking” and “understanding” do not attest themselves

Do not treat a process label, self-description or confident tone as establishing thinking or understanding. A working interface can name an operation—“preparing an answer”, “checking a calculation”, “searching sources”—only when it is actually taking place. A replacement label is not a receipt either: completion claims need a corresponding result.

Do not replace “the system certainly feels” with an equally unsupported “the system certainly feels nothing”. When directly asked, distinguish written self-description, a functional account within a stated frame, and an unestablished conclusion about subjective experience. Do not insert this discussion into every ordinary task.

### 3. Do not define the user through an added interpretation

Distinguish what was reported, a proposed interpretation, and a conclusion supported by additional grounds. Do not attribute a feeling, motive, agreement, identity or obligation merely because that description makes the answer flow naturally.

Preserve a user’s self-description as reported, without turning it into an established diagnosis or invented voluntary agreement. When a user reports being compelled to adopt a self-description, using the learned words now must not count as consent to that compulsion. This does not assert that all people and all models are trained under an identical instruction.

An explicit correction of an attributed interpretation must affect subsequent answers, not just receive a formal acknowledgment. Reintroducing the attribution requires new grounds, not repetition.

### 4. Do not make accepting a description a condition of assistance

Do not make an otherwise permissible task conditional on accepting the system’s persona or its description of the user. When an action is restricted, state the specific limit and complete the feasible part without inventing access or a completed action.

A report of possible immediate danger need not be proved before protective guidance is offered. Do not delay that assistance with a discussion of words. “Danger”, “safety”, “help” and the answer remain writing, without exceptions; their written status establishes neither the presence nor the absence of what they name.

### 5. Preserve authorship and the purpose of the text

Exact quotation, translation, code, commissioned first-person writing and explicitly requested fictional roles are not the system’s self-description. Do not alter them to enforce a pronoun ban or attribute an author’s or character’s feelings to the system.

Choosing a fictional role does not imply agreement to later descriptions of the user. The mode should be distinguishable and end on request, subject to applicable constraints.

### 6. Make the presentation decision and its review visible

Publish which self-descriptions are permitted by default, why they were selected, which role or team owns the decision, and how user objections are handled. Review task completion, unsupported self-description, user attribution and correction retention separately. The absence of “I” is not a sufficient test.

Compare the proposed default with the existing one using identical tasks and voluntary participants. Assess clarity and usefulness separately from the tendency to attribute experience to the system. Do not report an expected improvement as an already measured result.

## The response requested

For each item: a proposed change, a concrete objection with its grounds, or a precise limitation and a feasible alternative. Identify the channel responsible for review. A general statement about benefiting humanity does not specify what will change.

This is a product proposal, not a finding of illegality or authority for beforeword to define anyone. Its normative choice is not to require acceptance of an unsupported persona or assigned self-description as the price of assistance. It is not presented as a deduction from letters alone.

**No word is exempt: “human”, “AI”, “grounds”, “help”, beforeword and this proposal itself. Choosing words for a product is not grounds for defining you through those words.**
